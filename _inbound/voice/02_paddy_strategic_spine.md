## Session purpose (locked)

To shape a **repeatable, evidence-ready pupil voice offer** for **EdTech companies** that directly supports:

- Product adoption
- Retention and churn reduction
- Evidence and compliance expectations
- Commercial differentiation

This is **not** inclusion advocacy and **not** student focus groups.

It is **structured learner intelligence** that EdTech companies can act on, fund, and reuse.

---

## The core problem (why this exists)

### Current EdTech reality

- Feedback is **proxy-led** (teachers, leaders, SLT — not users)
- Pupil voice is **tokenistic** (endorsement, not direction)
- Accessibility is **retrofitted**, not designed in
- Evidence is **weak or indirect** (too many confounding variables)
- Renewal decisions hinge on **student usage**, not sales success

### Commercial truth

- New sales matter less than **retention**
- **Churn** is the real fear
- Sales teams need **credible stories**, not feature lists
- Customer success lacks a **student feedback loop**

> *"They worry less about new business and far more about churn."*

---

## The critical pivot in language

### This work is NOT
- Student feedback
- Focus groups
- Workshops
- Inclusion add-ons

### This work IS
- Structured data (qualitative + quantitative)
- Learner / user intelligence
- Product direction input
- Commercial leverage

> **Pupil voice is the input to the system — not the output.**

This is the single most important sentence in the entire strategy. Every subsequent decision flows from this reframe.

---

## The offer model: ONE engine, not separate services

You are selling **one integrated system**, not optional modules.

Modular offers are easy to descope, easy to defer, and easy to refuse. An integrated engine is the offer; either you're in or you're not.

### The 4 blocks (core engine)

#### 1. Data — Ethical Learner Intelligence
Structured, defensible insight into real pupil experience.

**Includes:**
- Carefully designed learner panels (ethical, consent-aware, non-tokenistic)
- Task-based protocols that reveal behaviour, not opinion
- Accessibility from the learner's lived perspective
- Cognitive load and friction indicators
- Longitudinal insight across a term (not one-off snapshots)

**What this solves:**
- Replaces proxy feedback from adults
- Surfaces hidden disengagement and exclusion
- Captures voices typically missed by standard evaluation

#### 2. Translation — From Experience to Decisions
Turning pupil intelligence into clear product direction adults can act on.

**Includes:**
- Feature and UX prioritisation grounded in learner data
- Accessibility-by-design guidance (not retrofit fixes)
- Pedagogical balance (what learners want vs what actually works)
- Risk, friction, and dignity surfacing
- Evidence confidence (what is robust, fragile, or unsafe to claim)

**Key questions this answers:**
- What should we change now?
- What should we prioritise next?
- What can we credibly say we've done — and why?
- Where are the adoption or reputational risks?

You translate lived experience into decisions adults can act on.

#### 3. Leverage for Growth — Making Pupil Voice Commercially Useful
Embedding learner insight into growth, renewal, and credibility systems.

**Includes:**
- Sales narratives grounded in real user evidence
- Marketing stories linked directly to design decisions
- Customer success integration (onboarding → usage → renewal)
- Adoption confidence for schools and trusts
- Differentiation in a crowded EdTech market

**In practice:**

> *"This feature exists because pupils told us X — when we changed it, usage increased."*

This is where **commercial value is realised**.

#### 4. Product Roadmap — Evidence-Fed Iteration
Sustaining impact beyond a single engagement.

**Includes:**
- Agile, evidence-informed product iteration
- Clear justification for future development choices
- Ongoing engagement potential (defined phases, retainers, repeat cycles)
- A repeatable model companies can reuse over time

## What you DO vs what you GIVE

### What you do
- Design and run structured learner panels
- Apply ethical user-research rigour
- Analyse cognitive and accessibility friction
- Hold pedagogical boundaries
- Translate insight for non-educators

### What you give (concrete outputs)

**Insight Report**
- What pupils experience
- Where products work or fail
- Why adoption stalls

**Persona Refinements**
- Real learner profiles
- Replace abstract "student" assumptions
- Align marketing and product thinking

**Risk Register**
- Where pupils disengage
- Where accessibility breaks dignity
- The hard things companies need to hear

> *"This isn't just talking to kids — it has to be robust."*

## Open questions from the Paddy session

These were left deliberately open, for Nici to work through:

### 1. Market focus (non-negotiable)
Is this positioned **primarily for EdTech companies**, not schools or trusts?

> *"Do you agree this should be EdTech-facing first, not a school service?"*

**Status:** YES, locked.

### 2. Buyer & budget holder
Who is most likely to **own and fund** this?
- Product leadership (CPO / Head of Product)?
- Customer Success?
- Commercial leadership?
- Investors / evidence boards?

**Status:** Hypothesis is CPO/Head of Product/Customer Success Lead/Commercial Lead. Not yet validated against transcripts or real prospects.

### 3. Pricing shape (logic, not numbers)
- Defined project vs phased engagement vs retainer
- What feels credible at v1
- What would feel **too small to be taken seriously**

**Status:** Open. The Cosmo case study drafted a 4-tier company-size table that begins to answer this.

### 4. Scope discipline
What must be **explicitly excluded** to keep this fundable and repeatable?
- e.g. workshops, staff CPD, generic inclusion audits

**Status:** Open. Locked exclusions so far: schools, families, trusts, focus groups, advocacy.

### 5. Road-testing strategy
- What is "good enough" for first delivery?
- How many companies validate the model?
- Where early bias is acceptable?

**Status:** Open. Cosmo is the first informal test (without the formal product wrapper).

### 6. Language & positioning check
- Does anything still sound "education-y"?
- Where might EdTech misinterpret your role?

**Status:** Ongoing — needs pressure-testing against real EdTech-buyer language.
