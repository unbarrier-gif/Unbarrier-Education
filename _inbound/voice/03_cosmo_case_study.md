## What Nici uniquely brought (her excellence + capacity in the room)

The transcripts make this very clear. Without her in the room, Cosmo would not have:

### 1. Pedagogical authority that translated buzzwords into classroom reality

- Caught **"lack of basic skills"** as triggering language and reframed it as **"foundation skills" / "school start readiness"** — *one phrase change protects the entire marketing position with teachers.*
- Named the missing concept: **"learning how to learn"** — Cosmo's team adopted it on the spot.
- Pushed back on putting setup complexity *in* the priority list:
  > *"It shouldn't be in that list. It's the foundation. If you put it in the list it makes everything else look down the list."*
  This reframed the entire prioritisation exercise.
- Gave them the language anchor: **"low floor, high ceiling"** for activity design.

### 2. Strategic positioning a non-teacher couldn't have given them

- **Phonics:** *"Don't mess with phonics. Schools have bought-in programmes and won't switch. Your gateway is Phase 1 — pre-reading sounds, rhythm."* This saved Cosmo from building features no school would buy.
- **Number Blocks (BBC):** *"Winner winner chicken dinner. Everyone uses it. Get the partnership."* Bradley immediately ran the AI search and confirmed.
- **Reading scheme partnerships:** flagged Read Write Inc, Little Wonder, Jolly Phonics, Bug Club as the buy/build/partner decision.
- **Twinkl + BBC Bitesize** as competitive benchmarks for guided reading content.
- **Skolon, GoLong, digit music, Apple, Jamf** as ecosystem context Cosmo didn't have.

### 3. Real classroom proof — "I've taught this age, this is what happens"

- Two/three-year-olds and herd mentality / structured rotations
- Calm reception drop-off → activity rotation as anti-dysregulation tool
- Why teachers want **"99.8% confidence"** before using a new tool
- Why TAs need scripted questioning prompts
- Why "randomisation" is wrong for early years (they need predictability first), but right for older year groups
- Why baseline assessment is locked-in-a-box and useless for actual teaching decisions

### 4. Sales/commercial framing in teacher language

- *"This data will talk to SLT."* — turned a feature decision into a procurement argument
- *"Talking points for parent-teacher meetings"* — gave them a marketing line
- *"The admin needs top-down view so the head teacher can see the value and buy in again"* — designed the renewal logic into the product
- Identified **"British Values / democracy"** as a curriculum hook for the voting feature

### 5. UX intuition from being a teacher AND being neurodivergent

- **"Just have one big button in the middle of the screen called Start."** Saved Bradley three iterations.
- *"For early years, set defaults — don't make the teacher choose. Customisation comes later."*
- *"Teacher profile settings are super smart for changing defaults."*
- Distinguished **"customise"** (theme/preset) from **"personalise"** (build your own) — solved a UI labelling confusion.
- *"My dyslexia means I need to talk things out. That's what kids need too — talk first."* Direct lived-experience design input.

### 6. The translator role no-one else could fill

This is the key one. Charlie (reception teacher) confirmed Nici's points but was junior, deferential, and gave granular classroom detail rather than strategic framing. Bradley gave product-build confidence but not pedagogy. The Cosmo team had no classroom credibility.

**Nici was the only person with both the lived classroom authority AND the commercial framing.** She translated between the room.

## What was MISSING from the workflow (the gaps unbarrier.voice would close)

This is the heart of the case study. Cosmo got Nici's brain in the room. They didn't get the product.

### Gap 1 — ZERO direct student voice

Across 6 sessions and the entire MVP design, **not one child was observed using a prototype.** All decisions were made by adults speculating about what children need. Nici flagged this implicitly throughout (*"talk to the kids," "watch what they actually do"*) but no one had a structured way to do it.

**What this means:** Cosmo is shipping an MVP designed by adults, with no behavioural data on how children actually interact with it. Their renewal conversation with schools next year will rest on: teacher anecdote and adoption metrics. Not student outcome.

### Gap 2 — No protocol, no instrument, no structured method

The sessions were brainstorming and feedback calls. Useful, but **not data.** No observation framework, no task-based protocol, no friction log, no cognitive load indicator, no accessibility audit from a learner perspective.

This is exactly what the Paddy work names as Block 1 of the offer — and it was completely absent.

### Gap 3 — No baseline

Cosmo's existing product has been in market for years. There is no baseline data on:

- Which existing activities children actually use vs ignore
- Which features cause friction
- Where accessibility breaks down
- What the cognitive load profile looks like

**Without baseline, the new MVP cannot be evaluated.** They will ship, observe usage, and tell themselves a story.

### Gap 4 — No deliverable artefacts between sessions

Nici was paid for time, not output. There is no:

- Insight Report
- Persona Refinement document
- Risk Register
- Decision log with rationale
- Pre-session brief or post-session synthesis

Which means: **Nici's value evaporates the moment the call ends.** It lives in someone else's notes (Manmeet's Granola transcripts) and in scattered bits of build. Six months from now, Cosmo cannot tell their investors *why* they made the decisions they made — because the reasoning was in the room, not in a document.

This is the single biggest missed-opportunity in the engagement. **Every session should have produced a tangible deliverable Nici could point to and Cosmo could hand to a board, a school, or an investor.**

### Gap 5 — No persona work

Cosmo's marketing still talks about "reception age children with SEND." Generic. After 6 sessions of rich classroom discussion (Charlie's class, the children Charlie described, the post-COVID generation Nici described), there should be 3–4 named personas with cognitive load profiles, accessibility needs, behaviour patterns, and use case scenarios. There aren't.

### Gap 6 — No risk register

Things Nici flagged as risks that have no audit trail:

- Free-flow play activities embedding misconceptions if unsupervised
- Phonics partnerships gating procurement
- Teacher confidence as adoption blocker
- Randomisation overwhelming early years
- Setup complexity killing the whole product
- Safeguarding for shared iPads / cloud data

If any of these become a problem after launch, no one will remember Nici warned them.

### Gap 7 — No staged process by company size

This engagement was a small/mid EdTech co-design. The model used wouldn't work for a 200-person EdTech with a real product team and procurement layers. **There is no documented variation of the process by company stage.** (See next section.)

### Gap 8 — No classroom face-to-face student voice testing

The natural next step — taking the Cosmo MVP into a real reception class, watching real children interact with it, returning structured data to Cosmo — was never scoped, costed, or proposed.

**This is the missing 30% of the engagement and the highest-margin part of unbarrier.voice.** Cosmo would absolutely buy this. They have no internal capability for it.

## What could have been leveraged as a deliverable BEFORE each session

This is the new playbook — what unbarrier.voice would deliver as part of the engagement:

| Session | Pre-session deliverable (1–3 days before) |
| --- | --- |
| **1. Intro call** | One-page "Discovery brief" — what we'll cover, what Nici needs from them, expected outputs. Sets professional tone from minute one. |
| **2. MVP Workshop Part 1** | Pre-read brief: EYFS framework summary, current Cosmo product accessibility audit (1-pager), 5 questions Nici wants answered in the room. Sent to all attendees including Bradley. |
| **3. MVP Workshop Part 2** | Morning recap (30-min written): what we agreed in the morning, what's open for the afternoon, MoSCoW pre-fill based on morning discussion. |
| **4. Guided Movement review** | Review brief: "What I'll be looking for" — UX checklist, accessibility checklist, EYFS appropriateness rubric. Sent 2 days before. Lets Bradley pre-empt issues. |
| **5. Mood Checker review** | Same template, populated for this activity. Plus: 3 specific questions about safeguarding/data architecture that surfaced from session 4. |
| **6. Guided Reading review** | Same template + cumulative "open issues" list from sessions 4 and 5. |

## What unbarrier.voice would deliver next for Cosmo (next steps)

If Nici went back to Cosmo tomorrow with a Phase 2 proposal, this is what it would say:

### Phase 2: In-classroom face-to-face student voice testing

**The pitch in one line:**
> *Your MVP launches in September. Right now you have zero behavioural data from actual children. Let me put it in front of 30 children across 3 reception classes over one term and tell you what you don't yet know.*

**What gets delivered:**

1. **Recruit + onboard 3 partner schools** (Nici's network, ethical consent, value-exchange = free product + free CPD session)
2. **Run task-based protocols** with target children — not focus groups. Children using the actual product to complete defined tasks while Nici observes (and where appropriate, records).
3. **Capture across 5 dimensions:** cognitive load, accessibility friction, engagement curve, dignity surfacing, social/peer dynamics in group activities.
4. **Term-long longitudinal tracking** — first session, mid-term, end of term.
5. **Deliverables:**
    - **Insight Report** — what children experience, where the product works/fails, why adoption stalls
    - **Persona Refinement** — 4 real learner profiles to replace the abstract "reception child with SEND"
    - **Risk Register** — where pupils disengage, where accessibility breaks dignity, the hard things Cosmo needs to hear
    - **Sales narrative pack** — "this feature exists because pupils told us X. When we changed it, usage increased."
    - **Customer success integration brief** — how to use the panel data in renewal conversations
6. **Feeds Cosmo's roadmap directly** for the next product cycle (the Block 4 — Product Roadmap deliverable).

### Why this is fundable for Cosmo specifically

- They are pre-revenue on Cosmo Grow. They need adoption proof for Year 1 sales.
- They have no internal user research function.
- Their existing customer success team (Susan-led) needs a learner feedback loop.
- Their narrative of "accessible / SEND-supporting" needs evidence — they currently have anecdote.
- They are heading into procurement conversations. Schools will ask: *show me the evidence.*

## What student voice supports / proves (the answer to the buyer's first question)

When a CPO or commercial lead asks *"why should I pay for student voice when I have teacher feedback already?"* — these are the answers:

### Student voice proves (commercially):

1. **That your product actually works for the user, not just the buyer.** Teachers buy. Children use. The renewal conversation hinges on use.
2. **Where churn risk lives.** Teacher loves a product + children disengage = renewal in 18 months goes badly. Student voice catches this 12 months earlier than usage analytics do.
3. **Which features deserve investment and which don't.** Teacher feedback says "more features please." Student data says "these three drive engagement, the rest are noise."
4. **Adoption risk before launch.** A feature can pass internal testing and fail when it hits a real classroom because of cognitive load no adult predicted.
5. **Accessibility credibility to regulators and procurement.** "WCAG compliant" is not the same as "works for the dyslexic 7-year-old." Student voice is the evidence the next procurement cycle will demand.
6. **Sales differentiation.** "This feature exists because 30 children told us X" is a category-killer line in a competitive pitch.

### Student voice proves (pedagogically):

1. **That the design respects the developmental stage.** Adults consistently overestimate children's processing capacity at MVP stage.
2. **Where dignity breaks.** Particularly for SEND and neurodivergent children — when a product makes a child feel othered, they disengage and so does the teacher.
3. **Which assumptions about "engagement" are real and which are wishful thinking.**
4. **Where the product enables learning vs distracts from it** — the line is invisible to adult observers.

### Why student voice is BETTER than the alternatives

| Alternative | What it gives you | What it misses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Teacher feedback** | Buyer perspective, classroom logistics, curriculum fit | What children actually do with the product |
| **Usage analytics** | What buttons get pressed | Why they get pressed, friction, dignity, cognitive load |
| **Internal user testing** | UX intuition checks | Real classroom dynamics, peer effects, adult support patterns |
| **External UX research firm** | Methodology rigour | Pedagogy, EYFS framework knowledge, classroom credibility |
| **Inclusion / accessibility audit** | WCAG compliance | Whether the product actually works for the 60% in the middle |
| **🎙️ unbarrier.voice student panels** | All of the above, integrated, with classroom + commercial framing | (Nothing — this is the missing piece) |
