## What unbarrier.voice is (one paragraph)

unbarrier.voice is a structured pupil-voice product designed for **EdTech companies** — not schools, not parents, not trusts. It converts real learner experience into decision-ready evidence that EdTech companies can use to reduce churn, justify product decisions, support renewal conversations, and differentiate commercially. The locked tagline is: *"I help you build products people actually use, understand, trust, and benefit from."*

This is **not** inclusion advocacy, student focus groups, CPD, workshops, or audits-for-schools. It is structured learner intelligence — qualitative + quantitative — that EdTech can act on, fund, and reuse.

The pivot that makes it work: **pupil voice is the input to the system, not the output.**

## The 4-block engine (locked structure)

### Block 1 — Data (Ethical Learner Intelligence)
Structured gathering of pupil experience.
- Learner panels (ethical, consent-aware, non-tokenistic)
- Task-based protocols (behaviour, not opinion)
- Accessibility from the learner's perspective
- Cognitive load + friction indicators
- Longitudinal (term-based, not snapshot)

**What this solves:** replaces proxy feedback from adults, surfaces hidden disengagement, captures voices usually missed.

### Block 2 — Translation (Experience → Decisions)
Turning pupil intelligence into clear product decisions adults can act on.
- Feature/UX prioritisation grounded in learner data
- Accessibility-by-design guidance (not retrofit)
- Pedagogical balance (what learners want vs what works)
- Risk + dignity surfacing
- Evidence confidence ratings (robust vs fragile vs unsafe to claim)

**Key questions answered:** What do we change now? What do we prioritise next? What can we credibly say? Where are the adoption risks?

### Block 3 — Leverage (Commercial Use)
Making pupil voice commercially usable.
- Sales narratives grounded in real user evidence
- Marketing stories tied to design decisions
- Customer success integration (onboarding → usage → renewal)
- Adoption confidence for schools/trusts
- Differentiation in a crowded market

**The killer line:** *"This feature exists because pupils told us X — when we changed it, usage increased."*

This is where commercial value is realised.

### Block 4 — Product Roadmap (Iteration)
Sustaining impact beyond a single engagement.
- Agile, evidence-fed product iteration
- Justification for future development choices
- Retainer / repeat cycle structure
- A repeatable model companies can reuse

## What's still open (the design work itself)

The six blocks below are where this project lives. Each needs filling in.

### 1. Foundation
- **Who exactly is this for?** Specific buyer profile — role, company stage, pain, budget-holding power. Hypothesis from Paddy: CPO / Head of Product / Customer Success Lead / Commercial Lead.
- **What's the problem in their actual words?** Not how Nici describes it — how they describe it to each other in board meetings.
- **What's Nici's specific "why" for this audience?** Different from the schools/families "why." This audience needs the commercial-credibility angle, not the lived-experience angle (yet).

### 2. Belief system
The convictions that make the offer make sense. This is the conversion engine — not features.

Draft starting beliefs (to test against transcripts):
- Pupils are users. EdTech keeps treating them as subjects.
- Proxy feedback is convenient and wrong. Adults systematically misread what kids do with software.
- Churn is a learner problem, not a sales problem.
- Accessibility designed in is cheaper than accessibility retrofitted.
- Evidence the regulator will accept ≠ evidence the school will accept ≠ evidence the kid actually generates. Smart EdTech needs all three.
- The companies that move first on this win the next procurement cycle.

### 3. The product — what it actually is, how it works
The 4 blocks (above) need fleshing out into something a buyer can read and understand in 90 seconds.

Need:
- A one-page diagram of the engine
- A step-by-step "first 90 days with us"
- What physically arrives in their inbox

### 4. The funnel + backend
Greenfield. Nothing in Paddy or the Feb prototypes touched this.

**Funnel hypothesis (to test):**
```
Authority content (LinkedIn, podcasts, keynotes)
    ↓
Landing page (vision.unbarrier.me/voice — rewritten)
    ↓
Discovery call (60 min, qualifying)
    ↓
Paid scoping engagement (week-long, fixed fee)
    ↓
Pilot project (one product, one term, fixed deliverables)
    ↓
Retainer / repeat cycle
```

**Backend questions to answer:**
- How are schools recruited for panels? (Paddy's parked "school network / register model")
- What's the value exchange for schools? (parked from Paddy)
- How are panels run logistically? (in-school visits? remote? hybrid?)
- Consent + safeguarding architecture
- Data infrastructure — where does learner data live, who can see it, how long?
- Insurance, contracts, IP
- What does Nici do herself vs subcontract

### 5. Pricing + packages
Paddy parked numbers deliberately. We need three things:
- **Pricing logic** — defined project vs phased vs retainer (which combinations?)
- **Anchor price** — what feels credible at v1 (Paddy's note: *"too small to be taken seriously"* is a real risk)
- **Tier structure** — entry pilot, full engagement, ongoing retainer

The Cosmo case study (`03_cosmo_case_study.md`) drafted a 4-tier table by company size — pre-MVP / MVP-Pilot / Scale / Enterprise. Use that as the starting point.

### 6. Why Nici (for THIS audience)
The schools/families pitch is "lived experience + 26 years in classrooms." That's not the lead for EdTech.

The EdTech-facing credibility stack is closer to:
- Apple Professional Learning Specialist
- 26 years inside the buyer's actual market (UK primary classrooms)
- Direct work with EdTech companies (Goodnotes, Jamf adjacent, Cosmo)
- Neurodivergent herself — knows where products fail in ways no UX researcher catches
- No conflict of interest with sales — independent voice
- Fine art background (design eye) + SEND specialism (rigour)

To structure into a credibility narrative that lands with a CPO, not a SENCo.
