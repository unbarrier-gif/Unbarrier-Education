00 · North star

The one sentence.

If every other line on this page had to compress into one thing, it's this:

Positioning line A held room for neurodivergent people sitting on an idea — because the loop breaks when someone else sees it.

Every other line on this page is a way to say that same thing, to a different person, in a different moment, at a different scale.

02 · Three voice frames

Three ways of saying the same thing.

Different audiences need different access points. Use all three — each in the right channel. Mix freely within a piece; don't mix across a single message.

A The loop for: people who recognise the pattern in themselves

Sharp, diagnostic, pattern-naming. Names the loop so clearly people gasp a little. This is the Cardiff-stall voice, the merch voice, the hook voice.

Best in: flyers · social hooks · t-shirts · first line of landing pages

B Permission for: any diagnostic stage — early, late, self-, undiagnosed-but-curious

Soft, disarming, counter-hustle. Gives people permission to stop performing a brain they don't have. This is the newsletter voice, the about-page voice, the thank-you voice.

Best in: newsletter · about pages · thank-you emails · long-form

C Witnessed for: isolated thinkers, people who work alone

Quiet, relational, about the power of another mind. Names what groups do that a diary doesn't. This is the sales-page voice, the why-group voice, the booking-confirmation voice.

Best in: sessions sales page · booking confirmation · community offer

The loop voice.

Use as headlines, flyers, social hooks, merch. All are pick-ups, not explanations.

Your brain isn't the problem. Your system is.
You're not stuck because you can't. You're stuck because you're doing it alone.
You plan. You start. You refine. You stall.
You don't need a planner. You need a room.
Your third notebook isn't going to fix this.
The thing you've tried seven times is trying to tell you something.
One idea. 90 minutes. You actually move.
Some ideas don't want a note app. They want other people.
You don't have a productivity problem. You have a witness problem.

Frame B · permission lines

Use in newsletter, about, long-form. Aim: make a neurodivergent reader — at any diagnostic stage — exhale.

A system, designed for you — not retrofitted from someone else's.
Messy is a feature. We built the room around it.
You're allowed to bring a mess.

You don't need to arrive clean. You need to arrive.
Not every loop is a failure. Some are maps.

Use on the sessions page, in why-group explanations, and in booking / session confirmations.

03 · The founder voice

How Nici shows up.

For any page, flyer, or pitch that needs a human face, use one of these. Never use a headshot without a line; never use a line without a name.

Micro-bio (one line, for signatures + flyer footers)

I'm Nici — diagnosed dyslexic at 10, ADHD & dyscalculia at 20, still looping. These are the sessions I wish I'd had.

Short bio (for session pages, about footer)

I'm Nici. I've spent years circling ideas I couldn't quite land — until I built the kind of room I wished I'd had. Loop Breakers is that room. I run most sessions; a few rotate to other neurodivergent practitioners who bring their own frameworks.

Longer bio (for about page, media requests)

I'm Nici, the founder of Unbarrier and Loop Breakers. I'm not late-diagnosed — I've known I think differently since I was ten (dyslexia at 10; ADHD, dyscalculia, and Auditory Processing Disorder at 20). What I didn't know, for years, was how to build around it. I'm creatively prolific and have spent most of my adult life looping on the same handful of good ideas. I built the thing I needed: small, slow, held rooms for neurodivergent adults to move one idea forward in 90 minutes, without the shame spiral that comes from not fitting the productivity systems we've been handed. I've worked as a creative consultant, an educator, and a design practitioner. Loop Breakers is the distillation of everything I've learnt about how our brains actually get unstuck.

05 · Objections & answers

What people won't say out loud — and the line that answers it.

Objection 1My idea isn't formed enough yet.
AnswerBring a mess. Bring a half-idea. Any stage is the right stage — that's literally the point.
Objection 2I don't want to perform in front of strangers.
AnswerYou don't have to share on camera if you don't want to. Chat works. Silence works. You set the shape.
Objection 3I've done coaching before and it didn't stick.
AnswerThis isn't coaching shaped for a neurotypical brain. It's sized differently — smaller ask, smaller step, one idea at a time.
Objection 4I can't afford it / is this another £2k course?
Answer£10 for a Tuesday seat — £5 access seat if that's still too much, no questions. Wednesday Guest Stages are £25 sliding (£15 / £25 / £40, you pick). Pay-as-you-go. Skip any week. That's the whole pricing — no upsell waiting behind it.
Objection 5Why groups? I'd rather have 1:1.
AnswerBecause other people's stuck ideas unstick yours. 1:1 is available too (£120) — but most people move further in a group than they expect.
Objection 6Is this therapy? I'm in/not in therapy.
AnswerNot therapy. Coaching — thinking, strategy, accountability, witness. Works alongside therapy, doesn't replace it.
Objection 7What if I'm the only ADHD/autistic/dyslexic one in the room?
AnswerYou won't be. The whole room is neurodivergent. Different shapes of it, same general terrain.

06 · What it isn't

The safety frame.

Neurodivergent people have been sold things. Say what this isn't, up front, to earn trust.

What it is

  • Group coaching
  • A thinking room
  • A held space to work out loud
  • Facilitated accountability
  • Pay-as-you-go
  • Neurodivergent-first by design

What it isn't

  • Therapy
  • Clinical / diagnostic
  • A course with modules
  • A masterclass or webinar
  • A productivity method
  • A subscription lock-in
Condensed line Not therapy. Not a course. Not a masterclass. A held room, for the kind of thinking that needs other brains in it.

Why 20?

20 is the number of people Nici can hold well. After that, it's a different kind of thing — good, but different. The scarcity is honest: it's a capacity ceiling, not a marketing tactic.

What "slow-pace WhatsApp" means (plain English)

09 · Voice rules

The things that make it sound like us.

Do — say it plainly. "A room where you're allowed to be messy" beats "a supportive, non-judgmental environment."
Do — use the word moved, not transformed, unlocked, levelled-up.
Do — name the loop. Patterns recognised = trust built.
Do — use specific numbers (90 min, 10 hard cap, £10 Tuesday / £25 sliding Wednesday) over vague promises.
Do — leave an explicit "out". "No pressure." "Say no." "Not for you? Fine."
Do — lowercase headers where it reads warmer. Sentence case > Title Case.
Do — British English spelling (programme, organise, behaviour).
Don't — sell the outcome of one session as life-changing.
Don't — stack testimonials in a wall. One, well-placed, does the job.
Don't — use medical/clinical language ("symptoms", "treat", "manage").
Don't — say "journey" or "community" empty of content. Define community every time we name it.
Don't — use the word "tribe". Ever.
Don't — use emoji as punctuation. A single one, occasionally, with intent, is fine.
Don't — use stock photos of people looking wistfully out windows. (We use placeholder blocks until we have real portraits.)
