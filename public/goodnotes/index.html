For teachers

Teacher workflow — start here

One simple rhythm that fits what you already do: prepare, teach, feed back. Goodnotes slots into each stage — it doesn't replace your core tools.

Prepare

  • Build in your private space first
  • Add one answer zone with the question written in
  • Move it to the class folder when you're ready

Teach

  • Use Follow Me to guide the pace
  • View by question to see every learner live
  • Hide and show what students see

Feed back

  • Write directly onto individual student layers
  • Pick a model answer, let clustering group the rest
  • You stay in the loop — always

Before you ask

Questions I get asked most

The honest answers. If yours isn't here, email me and I'll answer it properly.

Is the training actually free?
Yes. Goodnotes funds it, so there's no cost and no invoice to your school. You're not buying anything and I'm not selling you a licence — Goodnotes Education is free for schools.
We're a trust with a lot of schools. Do we need a separate admin console for each one?
No. Each school can be set up as its own organisation inside one Goodnotes Education account, keeping its own email domain, while your central trust account manages and switches between them from a single Admin Console. You can start with a handful of schools and add the rest later.
Do we need iPads? What about Chromebooks and Windows?
Goodnotes is at its best on iPad with an Apple Pencil, because handwriting is the point. It also runs on Mac, Windows, Android and the web — so mixed estates work, they just work differently. Tell me what your estate looks like and I'll tell you straight what you'll get out of it.
We're a Google school. Does that matter?
Not really. Goodnotes sits alongside Google Workspace rather than replacing it — Docs and Classroom keep doing what they do, and Goodnotes handles the handwritten, visual, work-it-out-on-the-page thinking that a text box can't. Sign-in works with your Google accounts.
How long until staff are actually using it — not just logged in?
The set-up is quick; the habit is the slow bit. Realistically: one session to get everyone in and confident with a first workflow, then a few weeks of one small routine repeated. Schools that stall are almost always the ones that installed it and told staff to explore. That's the whole reason this page exists.
What about pupils with SEND, dyslexia, or slow processing?
This is my actual specialism — I'm dyslexic, ADHD and dyscalculic, and I spent 26 years in classrooms as a SEND specialist. Handwriting, colour, layout and the freedom to draw a thought rather than type it are exactly what a lot of neurodivergent learners need. But a tool doesn't do inclusion on its own; how you use it does. That's the conversation.
What happens after the free session — do you then try to sell me something?
Only if you want it. The free conversation and the Goodnotes-funded training stand on their own. If you decide you want deeper inclusion work afterwards — an accessibility audit, ongoing advisory, classroom modelling — that's separate paid consultancy, and I'll say so plainly rather than sliding it in.
